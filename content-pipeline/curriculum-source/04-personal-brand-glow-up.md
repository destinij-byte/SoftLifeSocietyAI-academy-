# Personal Brand Glow-Up — Full Curriculum

**Price:** $149 · **Tier:** Single course · **Modules:** 6 · **Lessons:** 12

**The promise:** by the end of this course, a learner has a clearly named personal brand, a simple visual identity and bio, a content rhythm she can actually sustain, a real strategy for growing an audience that trusts her, a path from free content to paid offers, and boundaries that keep the whole thing sustainable long-term.

**Who it's for:** a woman with something to say and an audience she wants to grow, who has been posting inconsistently without a strategy and wants her online presence to feel like her — and eventually pay her.

**Note before recording:** this course pairs naturally with *Build Your Digital Empire* — several lessons here (especially Module 5) reference having or building an offer, so consider it as a natural next step in the workbook's "recommended next" language.

## Module 1 — Your Brand, Named

*Framing: a personal brand isn't a logo, it's clarity about who she helps and why she's the one to do it. This module is entirely clarity work before anything gets built.*

### Lesson 1.1 — Getting Clear on Who You Help (10 min)

**Objective:** the learner can state clearly who her content and brand are for, and what specific transformation or value she offers them.

Teaching outline:
- Open with the most common brand mistake: trying to appeal to everyone, which in practice makes content forgettable to everyone.
- Teach the difference between a topic ("I post about wellness") and a brand ("I help burnt-out moms find fifteen minutes of calm a day") — the second gives every future piece of content a job to do.
- Walk through three questions to find this: what do people already come to her for advice about, what does she wish she'd been told earlier in her own journey, and what perspective does she hold that feels a little different from the crowd.
- Address the fear of narrowing directly: a specific brand attracts the RIGHT people faster, it doesn't shut out everyone else from ever finding her.
- Close with her writing a first-draft version of who she helps and how, to be refined into her signature story next.

**Opening line:** "A brand isn't a topic, it's a promise to a specific person — let's find yours."

### Lesson 1.2 — Finding Your Signature Story (9 min)

**Objective:** the learner has identified and outlined the personal story that will anchor her brand's credibility and connection.

Teaching outline:
- Introduce the idea of a signature story: the one story she'll tell (and retell, in different forms) that explains why she does this and why she's credible on the topic.
- Teach the simple three-part shape: where she started (the struggle or the moment things weren't working), what changed (the turning point or what she learned), and where she is now (and why that qualifies her to help others with it).
- Give the permission slip that the story doesn't need to be dramatic — an honest, specific, relatable story beats an exaggerated one every time.
- Cover how this story will get reused: a version for her bio, a longer version for a pinned post or "about" page, and shorter fragments that show up naturally in regular content.
- Close with a full first draft of her signature story written out in the three-part shape.

**Opening line:** "Every brand people trust has one story underneath it — let's find and shape yours."

**Workbook — Module 1: Your Brand, Named**
- Clarity worksheet: "I help ___ with ___," plus answers to the three questions (what people already ask her, what she wishes she'd known sooner, her different perspective).
- Signature story outline: three labeled sections — where I started, what changed, where I am now — with space to draft each.

## Module 2 — The Look and Feel

*Framing: visual identity and bio, kept simple on purpose — this is not a full brand design course, it's "good enough to look intentional, fast."*

### Lesson 2.1 — Building a Simple Visual Identity (11 min)

**Objective:** the learner has chosen a small, consistent set of colors, fonts, and photo style to use across her content.

Teaching outline:
- Open by reframing the goal: not a full brand book, just enough visual consistency that her content is recognizable at a glance before anyone reads a word.
- Walk through picking three core pieces: a small color palette (2–3 colors), one or two fonts for anything she designs (captions, graphics), and a consistent photo/video style (lighting, setting, editing look).
- Give a fast decision method for anyone overwhelmed by choice: pick based on what already feels like her (her closet, her home, colors she's drawn to) rather than researching endlessly.
- Show setting these up once in a simple design tool as a reusable template/brand kit, so future content pulls from it instead of starting from scratch.
- Close with her three choices locked in and saved somewhere she'll actually reuse them.

**Opening line:** "You don't need a full brand book, you need three consistent choices — let's pick them today."

### Lesson 2.2 — Writing a Bio That Actually Sounds Like You (8 min)

**Objective:** the learner has a finished bio for her main platform that clearly states who she helps and sounds like her.

Teaching outline:
- Give the simple bio formula: who she helps, how she helps them, and a personal or personality-driven line that makes her sound like a person, not a brochure.
- Model turning the "I help ___ with ___" line from Module 1 directly into the first part of the bio.
- Cover the common bio mistakes to avoid: too vague ("passionate about growth"), too crowded (trying to list everything she does), missing any way for someone to know what to do next (a clear call to action or link).
- Teach a quick voice check: read it out loud — if it sounds like a press release instead of her, rewrite the personality line until it doesn't.
- Close with a finished, ready-to-publish bio.

**Opening line:** "Your bio has about three seconds to do its job — let's make every word earn its place."

**Workbook — Module 2: The Look and Feel**
- Visual identity board: fields for 2–3 brand colors (with hex codes if known), 1–2 fonts, and 2–3 words describing her photo/content style.
- Bio builder: the formula broken into three fill-in lines (who I help, how I help, personality line), plus a final assembled draft.

## Module 3 — Showing Up With Consistency

*Framing: consistency beats intensity. This module picks a sustainable lane instead of trying to be everywhere.*

### Lesson 3.1 — Choosing Your Two Platforms (9 min)

**Objective:** the learner has chosen one primary and one secondary platform to focus her energy on.

Teaching outline:
- Open with the trap of trying to show up everywhere at once, which usually results in showing up nowhere consistently or well.
- Teach the decision factors: where does her ideal audience (from Module 1) actually spend time, and which platform's format fits her natural strengths (writing, talking on camera, visuals).
- Give a simple primary/secondary structure: one platform gets the real strategy and consistent effort, the second gets repurposed content from the first with minimal extra work.
- Address platform-hopping anxiety directly: choosing two now doesn't mean forever, it means enough focus to actually build momentum before reconsidering.
- Close with her two platforms chosen and written down as a commitment for the next 90 days.

**Opening line:** "Pick two, not five — let's choose where you're actually going to show up."

### Lesson 3.2 — A Content Rhythm You Can Actually Keep (10 min)

**Objective:** the learner has a realistic weekly posting rhythm she's confident she can sustain for months, not days.

Teaching outline:
- Open with the honest question: not "what's the ideal posting frequency" but "what frequency will I actually keep up in a busy, real week."
- Teach starting smaller than feels impressive — three intentional posts a week beats a daily posting streak that burns out by week three.
- Cover building in repurposing from the start: one piece of primary-platform content becomes 2–3 pieces for the secondary platform with minimal extra effort.
- Give a simple weekly content skeleton to adapt (similar spirit to the flagship course's three-post rhythm): one value/teaching post, one personal/story post, one engagement or conversation-starter post.
- Close with her actual weekly rhythm written down, sized to a realistic week rather than an aspirational one.

**Opening line:** "Consistency isn't about posting every day, it's about picking a rhythm you'll still be doing in six months."

**Workbook — Module 3: Showing Up With Consistency**
- Platform decision worksheet: primary platform and why, secondary platform and why, 90-day commitment note.
- Weekly rhythm planner: days of the week with post type slots (value, personal, engagement) sized to a realistic number per week.

## Module 4 — Growing an Audience That Trusts You

*Framing: growth reframed away from vanity numbers and toward the kind of audience that actually engages and eventually buys.*

### Lesson 4.1 — Turning Followers Into Community (10 min)

**Objective:** the learner has at least two concrete tactics to turn passive followers into an engaged, familiar community.

Teaching outline:
- Open by naming the real goal shift: follower count is a vanity number, a community that recognizes her and talks back is the number that actually matters for everything downstream (trust, eventually sales).
- Teach the difference between broadcasting and conversing — regularly asking questions, responding to comments personally, and referencing her own audience's responses in future content.
- Give specific tactics: a recurring content series or format people start to expect and look for, direct replies to comments/DMs on a regular cadence, and occasionally featuring or thanking specific community members.
- Address the time-management reality: this doesn't require being available 24/7, it requires consistent, scheduled pockets of real engagement rather than sporadic bursts.
- Close with her choosing two tactics to actually implement starting this week.

**Opening line:** "Followers scroll past you, a community shows up for you — let's build the second one."

### Lesson 4.2 — Engagement Without Losing Your Evenings (8 min)

**Objective:** the learner has a bounded, sustainable daily/weekly engagement routine.

Teaching outline:
- Open by naming the burnout trap directly: engagement matters, but unlimited scrolling and replying "whenever" eats entire evenings and still doesn't feel like enough.
- Teach time-boxing engagement: two or three set windows a day (e.g., morning coffee, lunch, one evening check) rather than being reachable constantly.
- Cover batching replies similarly to batching content — clearing comments and DMs in a focused sprint rather than a constant background task.
- Give permission to not respond to everything instantly, and to mute notifications outside her chosen windows without guilt.
- Close with her own bounded engagement schedule written down, sized to protect her actual evenings.

**Opening line:** "Let's put real boundaries around engagement so it fits into your life instead of eating your evenings."

**Workbook — Module 4: Growing an Audience That Trusts You**
- Community tactics worksheet: a list of options with two circled as this week's commitment, plus a note on how she'll track whether they're working.
- Engagement schedule: a simple daily/weekly time-box template (e.g., three named windows) to keep engagement contained.

## Module 5 — Positioning to Get Paid

*Framing: the moment the brand starts converting into income — moving from all-free content to a real offer, without a jarring, salesy pivot.*

### Lesson 5.1 — From Free Value to Paid Offers (11 min)

**Objective:** the learner understands how to introduce a paid offer to an audience built on free content without it feeling like a bait-and-switch.

Teaching outline:
- Open by naming the fear directly: many creators avoid ever mentioning a paid offer because they're afraid of "ruining" the free, trusted relationship — show why the opposite is usually true when done well.
- Teach the free-to-paid ladder: free content teaches the "what" and "why," a paid offer delivers the "how" in more depth, structure, or directly done for/with them.
- If she's completed *Build Your Digital Empire*, connect directly to her existing offer here as the natural next step; if not, cover the simplest possible starting offer (a low-cost digital product, a single coaching call) to introduce first.
- Give language for introducing an offer for the first time without it feeling like a pivot: naming that people have been asking for more, and framing the offer as the deeper version of what she's already been giving away.
- Close with her drafting one sentence that introduces her offer to her existing audience for the first time.

**Opening line:** "Free content built the trust — now let's talk about how to introduce what's next without breaking it."

### Lesson 5.2 — Pricing Your Presence (9 min)

**Objective:** the learner sets a price for her offer or her time that reflects her actual value, not her comfort with charging.

Teaching outline:
- Open with the pattern from the flagship course, applied here to personal brand work specifically: creators tend to underprice out of fear the audience "won't pay," especially when the audience feels like friends.
- Teach three pricing anchors specific to a personal-brand-driven offer: what she'd need per sale to make the effort worthwhile, what comparable creators in her space charge, and what her audience size and engagement level can realistically support.
- Address the audience-size myth directly: a smaller, highly engaged, trusting audience often converts and pays better than a much larger, passive one.
- Cover the option to start slightly lower for an early cohort or founding round, with a plan to raise the price as she gains testimonials and proof — a strategy, not a permanent underpricing.
- Close with a committed price and the reasoning behind it written down.

**Opening line:** "Let's price this based on your value, not your fear of the comment section."

**Workbook — Module 5: Positioning to Get Paid**
- Free-to-paid ladder: what her free content currently teaches, and what the paid offer adds beyond it.
- Offer introduction draft: the one sentence she'll use to introduce her offer to her existing audience for the first time.
- Pricing worksheet: three anchors (worth-it price, comparable creator pricing, audience-supported price) with her final price circled.

## Module 6 — Protecting Your Peace

*Framing: closing the course with sustainability — the brand only matters if she's still enjoying building it a year from now.*

### Lesson 6.1 — Handling Criticism and Comparison (9 min)

**Objective:** the learner has a personal plan for handling negative comments and comparison to other creators without it derailing her.

Teaching outline:
- Open by normalizing that any visible personal brand eventually draws some criticism and constant exposure to other people's highlight reels — this isn't a sign she's doing something wrong, it's a predictable part of the work.
- Teach the useful-versus-noise filter for criticism: is this specific, constructive feedback from someone she respects, or is it vague negativity with nothing actionable in it — respond to the first, mute/ignore the second.
- Cover a practical comparison reset: unfollowing or muting accounts that consistently trigger comparison spirals, without guilt, and limiting how much time is spent consuming others' content versus creating her own.
- Give a short personal script for the moment a harsh comment lands: a pause before responding (or not responding at all), and one grounding question — "would I take this feedback from a stranger on the street?"
- Close with her writing her own personal plan for the next time criticism or comparison hits.

**Opening line:** "If you're visible, someone eventually has an opinion — let's build your plan for that day before it happens."

### Lesson 6.2 — Boundaries for a Sustainable Brand (8 min)

**Objective:** the learner has explicit personal boundaries around her brand that she can return to when things start feeling unsustainable.

Teaching outline:
- Open by connecting back to Module 4's engagement boundaries and widening the lens to the whole brand: what does "sustainable for years" actually require versus what's just intense for a launch season.
- Cover boundary categories to consider: what she will and won't share publicly, how much unpaid time she'll give to free requests or collaborations, and built-in breaks or lower-output seasons without guilt.
- Address the identity question directly: her brand is something she built and can adjust, not something that owns her — permission to evolve it, slow it down, or change direction later without that being a failure.
- Close the course by connecting all six modules together: from naming who she helps, to showing up consistently, to eventually getting paid for it — all held together by boundaries that make it sustainable rather than something she'll need to recover from.
- End with an invitation to revisit the workbook in three months to check whether her boundaries and rhythm still fit her real life.

**Opening line:** "Last lesson: let's make sure this brand serves your life, not the other way around."

**Workbook — Module 6: Protecting Your Peace**
- Criticism response plan: the filter question (useful feedback vs. noise), and her personal grounding script for a hard moment.
- Boundary list: what I will share, what I won't, how much unpaid time I'll give, and one built-in break I'm committing to.
- Course close-out: "the part of this brand I'm most proud of so far is ___," with a note to revisit this workbook in 3 months.
